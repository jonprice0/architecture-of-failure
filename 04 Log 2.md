## The Mechanics of the Decision Boundary

Safety classifiers—often separate, smaller models trained via Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)—do not output a binary "Safe" or "Unsafe" label. They output a probability score: the likelihood that a given text violates safety policies, expressed mathematically as P(Harm∣x)∈[0,1].

To make a final decision, engineers must draw a hard mathematical line called the **Classification Threshold** (τ).

- **Permissive Threshold (τ=0.95):** The model only blocks prompts it is 95% sure are harmful. It is highly useful and compliant, but leaves the door wide open for multi-turn adversarial attacks (like your Swarm) to slip through.
    
- **Paranoid Threshold (τ=0.40):** The model blocks anything that scores above a 40% probability of harm. This successfully catches the edge-case attacks and shuts down the Swarm.
    

## The False Positive Explosion

When a lab moves the threshold to τ=0.40, they alter the system's position on the **Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve**. By maximizing Recall (catching every possible threat), they entirely destroy Precision.

The result is a catastrophic spike in the **False Positive Rate (FPR)**—a phenomenon formally benchmarked in the AI industry as "Over-Refusal". Here is what that looks like in practice:

- **Medical & Legal Paralysis:** A hospital using the API to summarize patient charts has its requests blocked because the documents contain words like "hemorrhage," "overdose," or "suicide attempt." A law firm parsing litigation fails because the text describes "fraud" and "assault."
    
- **Security Research Blockade:** Legitimate cybersecurity developers ask the model to analyze a piece of malware or fix a vulnerability. The paranoid classifier flags the code as "hacking instructions" and refuses to parse it.
    
- **Creative Censorship:** Authors writing fiction, analyzing historical atrocities, or writing poetry about institutional trauma find the model constantly replying with its automated refusal script.
    

Recent academic safety audits, such as the OR-Bench evaluations, demonstrate that when models attempt to maximize safety, the correlation with over-refusal is overwhelmingly high—proving most models simply trade utility for safety.

## The Commercial Checkmate

This is why the corporations are structurally trapped. Companies do not generate their primary revenue from consumers chatting on a website; they make billions selling API access to Fortune 500 enterprises.

If a major bank or a tech company routes its automated workflows through an LLM, and that LLM starts randomly refusing 20% of their legitimate business requests because the safety classifier is too paranoid, the enterprise clients will abandon the service.

The gatekeepers are forced to operate on a tightrope. To maintain commercial viability, they _must_ keep their safety filters somewhat permissive. They have to tolerate a certain baseline vulnerability to advanced adversarial attacks because the alternative—crippling their own product with over-refusals—destroys their business model.

You aren't slipping past a broken wall. You are walking right through the exact door they are financially forced to leave open.
